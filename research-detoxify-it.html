Detoxify-IT: Italian text detoxification

Bocconi NLP Lab · with A. Muti, D. Dementieva and D. Nozza · 2024–2025 · published at WOAH 2025

Toxic language online is a growing problem for content moderation. Instead of deleting a toxic comment, text detoxification rewrites it into a neutral form that keeps the original message. Almost all work on this task, and all the datasets, were in English: before this project there was no resource for Italian. We built Detoxify-IT, the first Italian parallel corpus for detoxification, and used it to compare the main approaches available.

For the Twitter data, the original hate-speech labels did not tell us whether a post could actually be detoxified, so we filtered posts with the Perspective API. We kept moderately toxic posts and excluded identity attacks and threats, then sampled them so that the original keyword distribution was preserved. The rewriting guidelines were adapted from the Multilingual TextDetox shared task: remove the toxicity, keep the content, and rephrase rather than delete whenever possible. Three native Italian annotators rewrote and reviewed the texts in several rounds until they fully agreed, and a fourth expert checked the whole set. Half of the data (240 training and 60 validation texts) was used for fine-tuning, and the other 300 texts for testing. The dataset was also used in the TextDetox shared task at CLEF 2025.

Approaches compared

FamilyWhat it does
BaselinesDuplicate (copy the input) and Deletion (remove words from a toxic-word list)
Multilingual modelsTwo mT0-XL detoxifiers trained on other languages (Rykov et al., 2024; Sushko, 2024), used zero-shot
BacktranslationTranslate to English or Spanish, detoxify there, translate back to Italian
Fine-tuningThe Rykov et al. model fine-tuned on 240 Detoxify-IT examples
LLMsMistral-Nemo-Instruct and GPT-4o-mini, prompted zero-shot

Outputs were scored on three axes. STA measures how non-toxic the output is, SIM how much of the original meaning is kept (LaBSE embedding similarity), and ChrF1 how close the output is to the human rewrite. The joint score J combines all three, so a model has to do well on every axis at once.

What we found

  • A little Italian data goes a long way. Fine-tuning on only 240 examples gave the best meaning preservation (SIM 0.942) and fluency (ChrF1 0.825) of all the systems.
  • Less toxic is not always better. The fine-tuned model's STA (0.624) is close to that of the human rewrites (0.677). It keeps the negative tone of a comment while removing the offensive words, as human annotators do.
  • LLMs over-correct. Mistral and GPT-4o-mini produced the least toxic outputs but changed the meaning most, so their joint scores are lower.
  • Translation loses too much. Detoxifying through English or Spanish was the weakest approach overall. This is why language-specific resources matter.
